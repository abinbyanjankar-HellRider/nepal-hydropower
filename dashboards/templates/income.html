Income and finance cost

Every figure here is an estimate, not a reported result. Production is modelled from installed capacity and assumed seasonal capacity factors, priced at each plant's verified PPA rate (or an assumed tariff, flagged), then scaled once per company to its last reported year of electricity sales. For a company scaled this way the forecast annual income equals last year's reported sales: the model re-spreads it across the four quarters and adds no growth and no new plants. Wet season is mid-April to mid-December, dry season mid-December to mid-April. Fiscal quarters start in Shrawan (mid-July).

Finance cost and the effect of lower interest rates

Implied rate = finance cost ÷ year-end loans (an approximation: the average balance is not reported). The loan is assumed to be repaid by a fixed share of the current balance each year. A lower rate raises profit after the company's own effective tax rate, so a company on a tax holiday keeps all of it and a taxed company keeps about three quarters. EPS uses paid-up capital ÷ 100 shares; book value per share grows by the retained part of the extra profit. The repayment and retention controls also drive the forecast EPS and book value in the table above.
